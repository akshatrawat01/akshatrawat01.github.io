Feed the Black Hole

Pick something, then press, drag and let go anywhere to toss it. A clock starts the moment it leaves your hand and stops when it crosses the event horizon. Aim straight at the hole for a quick meal, or throw it sideways and watch it spiral for a while first.

Eaten 0 Escaped 0 Fastest - Longest - Horizon size 1.00x

    What's going on: the glowing ring is an accretion disk, gas that slows whatever passes through it, which is why sideways throws eventually spiral in. Close to the hole, tidal forces pull harder on the near side than the far side, so stars, planets and moons get stretched into streams (yes, the real term is spaghettification). A neutron star is so dense it stays in one piece. Each meal adds mass, and a black hole's horizon grows in direct proportion to its mass. One cheat: from far away you would never actually see anything cross the horizon, it would just slow down, redden and fade. The clock here is the falling object's own.
